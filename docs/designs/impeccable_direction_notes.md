## Seven grounded directions, ordered before the corrected draw

1. **Repertory cinema.** Film-club programme identities make an ensemble and ongoing work feel worth returning to. Deep cobalt shell, lemon-yellow wayfinding, clean white reading surface; assertive grotesk navigation and a readable literary serif; one signature horizontal chapter-entry rule ties the reading position to the cast panel. Familiar Story/Director tabs and controls stay ordinary.
2. **Illustrated atlas.** Cartographic publication systems support orientation within a world while keeping prose central. Lapis, restrained vermilion, and cool white; humanist serif prose and plain sans labels; a fine indexed reading-position margin is the signature, with no invented map feature or cartographic ornament.
3. **Woven anthology.** Cloth bindings and woven color samples provide material warmth without cream-paper nostalgia. Petrol-blue and saturated saffron shell accents, pale cool reading ground, dark ink prose; sturdy serif and clear sans; narrow interlaced color rules connect role-labelled exchanges and portrait captions, without a textured costume or decorative pattern field.
4. **Independent comics separation.** Two-ink graphic systems make different voices legible while preserving continuous paragraphs. Dark aubergine-free oxblood, coral, and ice blue with a white reading field; compact grotesk controls and a substantial readable serif; two offset color hairlines distinguish player contribution and writer continuation without speech-bubble fragmentation.
5. **Dance-company season identity.** Contemporary performance graphics give a character ensemble energy without making the interface a theatre prop. Graphite, pale peach, and saturated tangerine; an expanded sans heading voice with restrained serif story text; a shared horizontal baseline lets portrait captions and the current passage align as the signature gesture.
6. **Herbarium plate typography.** Collection labels offer precise names, portraits, and a calm long reading measure. Chlorophyll green, pale mint, and bone-white with ink-dark text; a robust book serif with ordinary sans controls; short aligned portrait captions are the signature, with no plants, antique paper, or imposed botanical genre.
7. **De Stijl anthology endpapers.** A reusable geometric color-role system can make a cross-genre shell recognizable without illustration or genre decoration. Warm red, royal blue, cream-free white, and charcoal; square-cut sans labels and highly readable serif prose; a narrow color-segmented navigation underline is the sole signature, while controls and page organization remain conventional.

The candidates span print/graphic, textile/material, and performance/collection traditions. All preserve a dominant story field, familiar Story/Director navigation, composer, and separate cast panel. No direction implies a new product feature or approved visual world.
